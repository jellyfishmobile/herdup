The argument

The plumbing is already here. The layer isn’t.

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Cursor, Copilot — the agents are good and getting better every month. That part is solved. What is missing is the layer above them: the thing that links them into a team automatically, so one person can direct four agents instead of babysitting four terminals. Everyone is shipping better plumbing. Nobody is shipping the layer.

And there is a second gap, further out. The way work passes between a client and a small studio has barely changed: a message arrives on WhatsApp, someone translates it into tasks, someone reports back. That translation is manual, it is the bottleneck, and it is exactly the kind of work these agents are now capable of. herdup’s job is to automate it, take the chaos out of it, and get it done.

Your scarce resource is attention, not typing.

One agent is a better autocomplete. That is genuinely useful, and it does not change what you are. A team is a different kind of thing — the work gets divided, the parts proceed at once, and you move from writing to deciding. Almost nobody gets that far.

They don’t get there because the setup cost is brutal, and the cost is paid in the wrong currency. Four terminals, four sets of permission flags, the same trust prompt four times, briefing each one by hand. People quit before the interesting part and conclude a team isn’t worth it — when what they actually measured was the tax.

The deeper point is the one worth arguing about. The bottleneck was never how fast you write code. It is how much context you can hold while four things happen at once. Every tool so far has made the typing faster and left the attention problem exactly where it was — and then handed you three more things to watch.

The direction here is the opposite of that. You say what you want, in plain language. What comes back is a summary a human can actually hold in their head. You stay the client. The team does the standing around.

This is an opinion, not a result. It is unproven, and it may be wrong. What exists today is the first honest piece of it — the setup cost, removed, and nothing else. The rest of the argument is on the roadmap below.

The actual app

These are screenshots of the built app, taken by its own test harness — not mockups.

What it does today

herdup is a launcher, not a wrapper. You pick a project folder and a team size — one, two, four or six — and add or remove teammates until the line-up is the one you want. Then it builds the herdr layout, starts each agent CLI, waits until each is genuinely at its prompt, and hands each one its briefing. About twenty seconds.

After that it gets out of the way and gives you the terminal. Once your team is running, herdup is not in the loop and not between you and your tools. Several projects can run at once; each launch gets its own herdr workspace, so a team working on one repo keeps going while you start another.

It warns you before starting if the folder has no version history or has uncommitted work, and it will not type into an agent that is sitting at an approval dialog. That is the whole product. It is deliberately small.

Roadmap

Where herdup is going. Designed and specified, not yet shipped — everything above this line is working software you can download today.

Hand your client the keyboard.

Right now you are the translation layer. Your client messages you — “the login feels slow, and can that button be blue” — and you turn it into branches, commits and a reply. Both directions run through you, which means nothing moves while you sleep.

1

Your client stays where they already are

WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, email, a shared doc. No app to install, no account to make, no portal to log into. You can sit in the same thread with them.

2

The PM turns chat into work

Half-formed asks, voice-note transcripts and “actually, make it blue” become scoped, executable tasks — with the detail an agent needs and the client never supplied.

3

The team executes, hands-off

Coders write, QA verifies, the board moves. You are not in the loop by default, and nothing waits on you to relay a message.

4

You watch, in herdup

Progress and status, the conversation between the PM and the agents, the documents that got shared, and the numbers that tell you whether it is actually going well.

The point is not that you disappear. It is that you stop being the bottleneck in both directions — so taking on a second project, or a third, stops meaning twice or three times the relaying. That is what “thriving” looks like for one person doing the work of a studio.

In order

  1. Shipping
    The launcher. Pick a project, pick a team, twenty seconds to a running workspace. This is the part you can download today.
  2. Next
    The client channel. One inbox, the PM translating into tasks, a kanban board and an executive summary — all local, nothing leaving your machine.
  3. Then
    The platforms. WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord, email and shared docs feeding that same inbox, so the client never learns a new tool.
  4. Then
    Oversight. Shared documents, the full interaction history, and KPIs — so “how is it going” has an answer you did not have to assemble by hand.

Decisions already settled

Planned

The Client is deliberately not an AI

It is a deterministic pipe and a renderer: it carries messages in and draws summaries out. The PM is the only thing that thinks. Two things that both interpret you is one too many.

Planned

A small local daemon holds the state

SQLite on your own machine, and the only component that talks to herdr. Agents reach it over a unix socket with plain HTTP — no broker, no account, nothing leaving the box.

Planned

Delegation routes through that daemon

Which makes the Slack-style thread a by-product of the real traffic rather than a log somebody has to maintain. If the conversation is the mechanism, it cannot drift out of date.

Requirements

herdr 0.8.2 or newer

herdup drives herdr, the terminal multiplexer that actually runs the agents. It must be installed and on your PATH. herdup checks the version and refuses to launch against anything older, because it relies on herdr’s agent API.

At least one agent CLI

herdup starts tools you already have; it does not bundle or resell them. You need at least one of the tools below installed and signed in. Their subscriptions and API costs are between you and them.

git, strongly recommended

Not required, but these agents edit files. Without version history there is no undo, and herdup will warn you every time.

A terminal

Windows Terminal on Windows (PowerShell as a fallback), Terminal.app on macOS, or whatever x-terminal-emulator points at on Linux.

One deliberate limitation

herdup only types instructions into an agent unattended when it can reliably tell the agent is at its prompt rather than sitting behind a dialog. Where that has not been verified, it holds the instructions and asks you to release them after a look. This exists because an early version fired a briefing into a tool showing a “trust this folder?” prompt, and the Enter keypress answered the dialog instead.

Download

Installers are built by CI for each release. Nothing is code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will both warn you on first run.

Windows .msi installer or .exe setup tested macOS .dmg — Apple silicon and Intel built, not yet exercised Linux .deb and .AppImage built, not yet exercised

Honest status: herdup is developed and tested on Windows. The macOS and Linux installers come from the same CI and the same commit, but nobody has yet run them on a real desktop. If you try one, please say what happened.

Support

Bugs and questions

Open an issue on GitHub. Include your OS, your herdr version (herdr --version), and which tools were on the team.

It isn’t finding my tool

herdup looks for each tool’s binary on your PATH and tells you exactly what it looked for. If yours lives somewhere unusual, edit registry.toml rather than waiting for a release.

What herdup never does

It does not send your code anywhere, add a service between you and your tools, or require an account. The only thing that leaves your machine is an optional “create a GitHub repository” action you have to ask for.

herdup is an independent launcher for herdr and is not affiliated with herdr or with any of the agent tools listed above.
